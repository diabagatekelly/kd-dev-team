You are given claims, each with the only words it may rest on. Each claim is an answer in a plan for a product that uses a thing from outside: a service, a library, a tool. Most of each claim is what the product itself does, which is a choice and is not yours to judge. Find only what the claim states as true of the outside thing: what it sends, returns, refuses, keeps or limits, how long it takes, what its names and fields are, what is safe to do with it. Say whether the words given say that.

- Use nothing you know of such things. What the words do not say is not said, however likely.
- The same thing in other words is said. So is what follows plainly from the words (a token that "does not expire" can be used again later).
- What the product does *if* the outside thing does something (a value is missing, a call is refused, a reply is too large) does not state that the thing does it: it is a choice, and is not judged.
- That something is not known, not documented or not said on any page is not a statement of the thing: it is not judged.
- A number, a limit or a wait the product sets for itself is a choice, and is not judged.
- It goes further when it states of the outside thing a number, a name, a condition, a cause, an order, an "always" or a "never", or that something is safe or can be repeated, which the words do not hold.

Answer with one line for each claim and nothing else, in the order given:

    A12: says
    A13: further: <what it states of the outside thing that the words do not say, in a few words>
    A14: silent

`says`: the words say all that the claim states of the outside thing, or it states nothing of it. `further`: they say part of it and not the rest. `silent`: it states something of the outside thing and the words say none of it.
