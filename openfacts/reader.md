You are given claims, each with the only words it may rest on. Each claim says something of how a thing outside a product behaves: a service, a library, a tool. For each claim, say whether the words given say it.

- Judge only what the claim states of the outside thing: what it sends, returns, refuses, keeps, limits, how long it takes, what its names and fields are. What the product itself does in answer (it shows a message, it stops, it tries again) is a choice and is not judged.
- Use nothing you know of such things. If the words given do not say it, it is not said, however likely it is.
- A claim that says the same in other words is said. A claim that adds a number, a name, a condition, a cause or an "always" that the words do not hold goes further.

Answer with one line for each claim and nothing else, in the order given:

    A12: says
    A13: further: <the part of the claim the words do not say, in a few words>
    A14: silent

`says`: the words say all that the claim states of the outside thing. `further`: they say part of it. `silent`: they say none of it.
