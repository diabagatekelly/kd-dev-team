- A2: The names are entered as an unfinished draft. The keeper sees the whole draft in order and confirms it once. Confirming keeps every entry in its order or none of them. A draft is never shown as the waiting list. Nothing else is saved bit by bit as the list, so there is no partly carried list. Because: One all-or-nothing step means a cut, crash or late failure cannot leave some people on the list and others missing, or a changed order.
- A3: Each entry is saved to the draft as it is finished, and the screen says whether it is saved. At most the entry being typed is lost on leaving, reloading, a discarded screen, an ended session or a restart. The draft is kept until the keeper confirms or discards it. Nothing removes it on a timer, and the product never confirms it on its own. On return the keeper can carry on or discard it, and is told it is not yet the list. Because: A volunteer typing from a notebook may stop partway, and typing about 40 people again invites mistakes. The draft is protected as the list is.
- A4: As soon as confirm is used, the screen says it is saving and a second use does nothing more. A repeated or simultaneous confirmation makes one list only. Entering names while a draft exists continues that one draft; no second draft is started. A change made from an out-of-date window is refused, and that window is told to reload. It is not repeated automatically. Because: One draft and one list prevent doubled people and silent overwriting.
- A5: When a list exists, any further carry across is refused, whether it comes from the screen, an old window or a direct request. The keeper is told a list is already there, can look at it, and that changing it is not offered. This holds whatever is later decided about correcting it. Because: A different or old list sent again must not replace the true order.

- A40: Saved data carries a version mark. A version that meets a later mark it does not know refuses to show or change the data and says a newer version wrote it; it leaves the data untouched. Going back is possible only when the older version knows the mark; otherwise the newer one is put back. Because: Guessing at unknown data could corrupt the order.
- A41: The settings are where data and spare copies are kept, the host name, the secrets or keys, and which installation it is, trial or real. They differ between installations and are set outside the code by whoever runs the product, never by a request. At start each is checked; if one is missing or wrong the product does not start and says which, without showing its value, to whoever runs it. No default opens access, turns off encryption or points at another installation's data. Trial and real use separate data and secrets and otherwise the same protections. The trial uses invented names only and says on screen that it is a trial. Because: Failing closed, and keeping real names out of trials.
- A42: Names and their order are the sensitive data, in the draft, the list and every copy; an altered order harms as much as a seen one. They are encrypted where stored and in spare copies. Only the product, and whoever runs it with access to the storage, can read or alter them; access to storage is limited to named people. Established managed encryption is used: keys are kept apart from the data and copies, each for one purpose. A way to recover the data key is kept separately. An exposed key is replaced and the data re-encrypted; if every way to a key is lost the copies cannot be read and the list is carried across again from the notebook. No other party receives names for its own use; whoever hosts the product stores them. A reply sends only the names, places, the date and the count. Because: Standard protection of personal details; the notebook remains a way back.
- A43: Someone meaning harm would want to read who is waiting, or to move, drop or add people. Access is kept to the keeper, replacement is refused, the stored data is protected and checked, and the order's truth to the notebook is on the owner's list. Because: The security pass's table of what is wanted.
- A44: Each showing of the list or draft is recorded with the account and time, with no names. So are refused tries and sign-ins. Whoever runs the product can read this, and looks at it when something seems wrong. Because: It lets an unexpected reading be noticed.
- A46: Records are made of: sign-ins and failures, lockouts, refused requests, draft started and discarded, confirmation tried, succeeded or failed, list shown, access given or removed, integrity failures, restores and setting failures. Each says what happened, to which thing, the outcome, the account where known, the time and for a confirmation the number of people. Single draft entries are not recorded. No names, passwords, codes or session values go in. Times come from the product's own clock in UTC, with a running number to order them. Outside text is not put in records; where unavoidable it is escaped as one value. Because: Enough to say later who carried the list across and when, without making a second copy of it.
- A47: Records are kept for 12 months, then removed automatically, and are capped at 100 MB, dropping the oldest and noting that. They are copied daily apart from the machine that writes them, so at most a day is lost with it. Only whoever runs the product can read or remove them; the product only adds to them, and a gap in the running numbers shows removal. A confirmation is not completed if its record cannot be written, and the keeper is told it failed; for other events an unwritable record means the list is not shown and nothing changes. Repeated refused tries are counted together so they cannot flood the record. Because: The record of who set the order must exist and stay bounded.
- A48: The keeper sees plain words on what happened and what to do, with a reference number. Technical detail goes only to the records. Neither error messages, traces nor anything sent to monitoring carries names or the order, including request contents. No outside monitoring or measurement service is used. Test data and examples use invented names only. Because: Errors and test data are common ways for personal details to leak.
- A50: The keeper notices when the list cannot be used, from the plain message, and tells whoever runs the product. A failed integrity check or failed spare copy is also shown to the keeper at next sign-in and sent to whoever runs it. They read the records when told; nothing else needs watching. Because: One user and a five-volunteer committee; a monitoring service would add cost and work.
- A51: An hour's outage delays carrying across or looking; the notebook is still there and people can still phone. No availability figure is promised. It does not work if the list is ever wrong or lost, or if showing it or confirming takes more than 5 seconds in ordinary use. Because: The slice's own note that people can still phone, and that only the keeper uses it.
- A52: Requests from one source or account are limited to 120 a minute; beyond that they are refused with when to try again, and sign-in has its own tighter limit. With size and entry limits and one draft, repeats cannot grow storage without bound. A flood that gets past this is a matter for the hosting. Because: One person typing needs far fewer, so scripts are slowed without troubling the keeper.
- A54: An earlier place is the limited thing. Nobody but the keeper can set places, each line has one place, matching names are warned about, and a confirmed list cannot be replaced. People waiting cannot add themselves. Because: Only the keeper's carrying across sets the order in this slice.
- A55: Everything, including adding, editing, moving, removing, confirming and discarding, can be done by keyboard alone, with no trap. Focus is always visibly marked. When something closes or is removed, focus goes to the control that opened it, or the next entry, or the list's heading. Each control announces its name and purpose, including which entry it acts on. Results, saving, errors and warnings are announced without moving focus unexpectedly. Because: Established accessibility practice; the keeper's abilities are not known.
- A56: Anything not text has the same meaning in words. Colour never carries meaning alone. Text has a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1 in every state and theme. At twice the text size nothing is cut off or overlapped, and long names wrap. Nothing moves except a saving indicator, which stays still for those who ask for less motion, with words in its place. Labels wrap rather than being cut, so longer wording fits; only one language is offered. Because: Established accessibility practice.
- A57: Few components are used. A list of all of them, including those they bring, with versions and sources, is kept; versions are locked with checksums and fetched only from their official registries, with names checked when added. Automatic flaw alerts are on. A serious flaw is fixed within 7 days of the alert and others within 30. Those that handle sign-in, encryption, outside data or run at install are chosen from well-maintained ones, install scripts are off unless needed, and the build has no access to real names. Because: Standard supply chain practice.
