Because: A person must not take an old copy for the current order, and the product has no way to read the notebook.

- A32: With nothing changed the product is closed: it needs sign-in, has diagnostic modes off and switches on nothing the criteria do not need. Settings can be changed only by whoever sets it up, never by a request or a page. A missing, empty, malformed or out-of-range setting that is needed stops the product from starting, with a message naming the setting, not its value, to whoever sets it up. Because: Safe defaults and refusing to start on a bad setting stop a mistake from opening the list.
- A33: Any trial copy, test data and example uses invented names only and never a copy of the real list. A trial differs from the real one only in its address, its secrets and its data. Because: The requirements say names are not to be shown to everyone, and a trial is less guarded.
- A34: A record is written for setup, each sign-in and wrong try, each lockout, password change and ending of access, each confirmed or discarded or removed list, each failed save and each failed check of the stored list. Each says what happened, which account, when by the product's own server clock in universal time, whether it worked, and for a list how many people; records are kept in the order written. Reading the list is recorded the same way, as that it was read. No name, password or session value goes into a record, and no text a person typed goes in at all. If the record of confirming or discarding a list cannot be written, that action is not carried out and the person is told it failed; other failures to record do not stop use. Because: These events show later what happened to the order without copying anyone's name into records.
- A35: Records are kept apart from the list with the daily copy, so the loss of one machine loses at most a day of them. Only whoever sets the product up can read them, and nothing in the product changes or removes them; their numbering shows a gap. They are capped at a size far above a year's use, with the oldest removed first and that removal recorded. Nobody reads them routinely: they are read when the list keeper reports something wrong. The list keeper is told on the screen of failures and wrong sign-ins as they affect them. How long records are kept is part of what the owner decides about keeping people's details. Because: A society of volunteers has no one to watch records, so what matters is shown to the list keeper and the rest is there when needed.
- A36: Names, places and the order never go into an error's message or trace, an error shown to whoever asked, or an address or its query. Nothing is sent to any monitoring or measurement service. A successful reply carries names only to the signed-in list keeper and only what the screen shows. Because: Keeping personal details out of errors, addresses and measurement is established practice.
- A37: Pages and replies that show names are marked not to be stored by browsers or anything in between. The product stores no names on the device; only the session value of A27 is held there. Because: This keeps the list off shared devices and intermediate stores.
- A38: What harms someone here is the names and their order (seen), the order (changed) and the sign-in values. The list, its unfinished form and every copy are encrypted where stored, with keys kept and managed apart from the data, each used only for that, readable only by the running product and whoever sets it up. Nobody else can open them. Whoever sets the product up keeps a spare of the keys somewhere separate. A replaced key is brought in without losing data; an exposed one is replaced and the data encrypted again; if all copies of a key are lost the list cannot be read and is brought across again from the notebook. The only other party that may hold the data is whoever the product runs on, which receives it to store and serve it and for nothing else. Because: Encryption at rest with separately held keys is established, and the notebook means a lost key does not lose the list.
- A39: Getting in as the list keeper to read the list is met by A23 to A27, and an attempt shows as wrong tries told to the keeper and recorded. A changed order cannot be brought across without the keeper's session and fresh password, over a kept list not at all, and the keeper checks it whole against the notebook before it is kept; confirmations are recorded. Someone taking over the way in is met by the same measures and noticed when the keeper cannot sign in or sees sessions or changes they did not make; the notebook remains, and recovery is what the owner decides. Because: These follow from the measures already chosen.
- A40: Everything can be done by keyboard, nothing traps it, and the focused item is clearly marked. When something is removed or closed, focus moves to the next sensible item. Every control has a name and purpose that a screen reader announces, results of saving and refusals are announced, and anything not text has words. Colour never carries meaning alone. Text contrast is at least 4.5 to 1 in every state, text enlarged to twice its size loses nothing, and nothing moves for someone who asked for less motion. The list is shown as a numbered list a screen reader can follow. Because: These are established accessibility measures, and many members are elderly.
- A41: The build keeps a list of every component, those they bring with them included, with where each comes from, at fixed versions checked against recorded fingerprints from the intended source. As few as possible are used, and those that see passwords, read what is typed or run at install are the ones reviewed first. Known flaws are checked for automatically. Who acts on a warning and how soon is part of what the owner decides about upkeep. Because: Pinned, listed and checked components are established practice.
- A44: The product takes only what the list keeper gives. A change in the notebook during the work gets in only if the keeper puts it in the unfinished list before confirming; the check at confirmation asks that it match the notebook as it is now. Because: The product cannot see the notebook, so the whole-list check is where such a change is caught.
- A45: The list keeper notices when they use it, because every failure is shown to them with what to do. Nothing watches it for them in this slice, and the notebook remains meanwhile. Because: Only the list keeper uses this slice, and watching would add cost and work for volunteers.
