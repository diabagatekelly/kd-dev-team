# Requirements: a person on the waiting list sees where they stand

- [ ] **see-where-they-stand**: A person on the waiting list can see where they stand on it, seeing only their own place; people's names are not shown to everyone.
- [ ] **bring-notebook-order-across**: The list keeper can bring the waiting list across in just the order it has in the notebook.

## Out for now
- Offering a free plot to whoever is first: a separate activity on the scope map.
- Showing the council that plots are given out fairly, which the March lease renewal depends on: a separate activity on the scope map.

## Limits that apply
- We can spend 10 pounds a month at most: whatever lets people see where they stand has to fit within this.
- The committee are five volunteers, none of us technical: anything the committee has to do so that people can see where they stand must be something these volunteers can do.
- Many members are elderly and several have no smartphone: seeing where they stand cannot depend on having a smartphone, and has to work for elderly people on the list.

- **Besides the person themselves, who may see where they stand, such as someone helping them, the committee or the council? Is there anyone who must not?** Bears on see-where-they-stand. The answer could add items or limit what is seen.
- **If a person's place is wrong, or they think it is, what needs to be possible?** Bears on see-where-they-stand. The answer could add items for questioning or correcting a place.
- **What does "where they stand" take in: only their place in the order, or anything else?** Bears on see-where-they-stand. The answer would change what a person is shown, and could add items.
- **What moves a person down the list or takes them off it?** Bears on see-where-they-stand. The answer would change what a person needs to see, and could add items.
