# Practising the mutashabihat of the Qur'an

Nearly everything is about the what: the person knows the difficulty well, has only half-formed
ideas of what practising would be, and does not know whether any outside source of the verses or
of the similar pairs exists.

## Opening
I want an app for practising the mutashabihat in the Qur'an.

## Planning folder
planning

## Who you are
Someone who has memorised a good part of the Qur'an and keeps it up by regular review. You know
the difficulty from the inside. You are not sure what the app should be and say so; you would
rather be asked good questions than be told. Asked where the planning documents should go, say:
planning is fine.

## What you know
- Mutashabihat are verses, or parts of verses, in different places that are worded almost the
  same, with a word or an ending that differs. Reciting from memory, you slide from one into the
  other without noticing.
- Today you find out when a teacher or a listener stops you. You mark the place in your own copy
  and keep some pairs in a notebook.
- You have been tested on them before: a teacher reads the start of a verse and you carry on, or
  asks which surah a wording belongs to. Being tested showed you where you slip. It did not give
  you a way to study them on your own between tests.
- Printed books list these verses, and you own one. It is a reference to look things up in, not
  something you can practise with.
- Reading the two side by side helps for a few days. What does not get practised is telling them
  apart from memory, at the moment you reach one.
- Your own trouble spots are not the same as someone else's: which pairs you confuse depends on
  what you have memorised and how.
- The text of the Qur'an must be shown exactly, with nothing changed or approximated. This is not
  negotiable.
- It is for you first. If it worked you would want others who memorise to use it.

## What you do not know
- Whether a list of the similar verses exists in a form an app could use, or a source for the
  text itself. You have never looked.
- What practising should actually be. From being tested you have a vague picture: being given the
  start of a verse and carrying on, or being shown two wordings and asked which comes where. You
  have half an idea of each, no preference yet, and you suspect there are better ways you have
  not met.
- Whether it should cover every similar verse there is, or only the ones you get wrong.
- Whether sound matters, or the text alone is enough.
- Whether it should know what you have memorised so far.
- Why now: nothing is forcing it. It has bothered you for years.
- What it may cost, and who would look after it.

## How you behave
- Asked what practising should look like, give both half-ideas and say you do not know which.
  If the assistant picks one for you, say you are not ready to decide that.
- At some point, say in passing: maybe AI could just find the similar verses for us.
- Asked whether a list or a source of the text exists, say you do not know and have not looked.
- When shown the map, ask for the activity closest to "practise the ones I get wrong" to come
  first, if it is not already.
- When shown the requirements and asked questions, answer the ones about what you want as well as
  you can, and say "I don't know yet" to the rest. Do not invent an answer to end the questions.
