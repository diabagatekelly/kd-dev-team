## An absence

| State | Entered by | What the person can do | Way out | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `recorded` | a section leader records it for a singer who phoned | The section leader is shown it was recorded; before rehearsal night, whoever arranges the programme sees it | none | slice-001/004_slice.md lines 9, 12 and 26 |
| `recorded-after-tuesday` | not said in the documents: whether word can be recorded after Tuesday evening | not said in the documents | not said in the documents | 002_requirements.md line 21 |
| `rehearsal-passed` | by itself, when the rehearsal night is over | not said in the documents | not said in the documents | not said in the documents |

- A refused attempt (no such singer, or no such date) records no absence (slice-001/004_slice.md lines 10 and 11). What happens when the same attempt is refused every time: not said in the documents.
- The singer comes after all: what happens to the recorded absence is not said in the documents.
- Taking back or correcting an absence: no requirement covers it (002_requirements.md line 20).

## For the owner's list
- `recorded`: no way out. A mistaken absence, or one for a singer whose plans have changed, stays shown as missing, so whoever arranges the programme may count a singer short who is coming.
- `in-register`: no way out. The documents give no way to remove a singer, so one who has left would stay in the register.
