# Requirements: find out before a rehearsal who will miss it

- [ ] **tell-of-absence**: A singer can let the choir know before a rehearsal that they will miss it.
- [ ] **record-for-singer**: A section leader can record an absence for a singer who phones them.
- [ ] **see-who-will-miss**: The person who plans the programme can see by Tuesday evening before a Thursday rehearsal, by voice part, who is known to be going to miss it, whether the singer sent word or a section leader recorded it, and which voice parts will be short, while the programme can still be rearranged.

## Out for now

- Working out who has missed three rehearsals in a row without a word, and asking them whether they're still coming: this is the next activity on the scope map. The code can already count the latest rehearsals in a row a singer did not come to, but no command shows it, and the count takes no account of whether the singer sent word.
- Already works: a singer can be added to the register with one of four voice parts (soprano, alto, tenor, bass).
- Already works: a singer who came can be ticked for a rehearsal date.
- Already works: the register shows, for a given date, who is ticked as having come in each voice part, and how many out of that part. It shows nothing about who will miss a rehearsal.

## Limits that apply

- Singers mustn't need a password, because several are in their eighties: a singer must be able to let the choir know without one. The register as it stands has no accounts.
- The register is on a small computer at the church hall that the section leaders use: the singers and their voice parts, which show who is in a part that will be short, are kept by the register there.

## Not yet confirmed

1. Word comes from the singer, and a section leader can record an absence for a singer who phones them. Can a section leader do so for any singer, or only for those in their own voice part? (record-for-singer: would change for whom it can be done.)
2. Does a singer who finds they can come after all need to be able to take their word back, and does a mistake need to be put right? The answer so far is "I think not, but I'm not sure". (tell-of-absence, record-for-singer: would add an item.)
3. Who needs to see who will miss a rehearsal: only you, or also the section leaders, the secretary or others? Is there anyone who must not see it? Is planning the programme your job alone, and what do you call your role? Not thought about yet. (see-who-will-miss: would change who it names, or add an item.)
4. Does a singer need to say in one go that they will miss several rehearsals, and how far ahead? (tell-of-absence, record-for-singer: would change what they cover.)
5. Does "miss" cover only being away for the whole rehearsal, or also arriving late or leaving early? (all three items: would change what is said, recorded and shown.)
6. Does a singer need to be able to see that their word got through, or what they have already said? (tell-of-absence: would add an item.)
7. Is there a rehearsal every Thursday without exception? If there are breaks, extra rehearsals or changed days, who decides them? (all three items: could add an item for keeping the rehearsal dates. The register holds no list of coming rehearsals.)
8. The absences need to be seen by Tuesday evening for the Thursday. Can word still be sent or recorded after that, and if so, what needs to happen then? (all three items: would change by when an absence can be sent or recorded, and what is shown.)

## Outside systems and lasting things

Outside systems: none.

Lasting things:
- A singer, with their voice part.
- A rehearsal.
- A singer's word that they will miss a rehearsal.
