## Chosen without asking; the owner can overturn any of these

- A6: when both rules refuse, nothing is recorded and the section leader is told of each thing that is wrong. Because: telling everything at once saves a second refused try.
- A7: recording an absence that is already recorded changes nothing; the section leader is told it was already recorded. Because: a second copy would count one singer twice and make a part look shorter than it is.
- A8: because a wrong absence misleads until someone notices and removes it, the singer's name, voice part and the rehearsal date with its weekday are shown and a yes is asked before saving. Every rule is checked before that and again at the save. Anything left before the yes records nothing. Because: confirming the details that could be wrong stops a slip before it changes the numbers.
- A9: the section leader is told it is recorded only after the save has completed and reached the computer's storage. Closing or losing power before that message means it must be looked for in the view and recorded again if absent. Because: a confirmation given before the save is complete can be false.
- A10: after an interrupted save the register holds either all that was there before or all of that and the new absence, never a part. Saved data is not added to or overwritten in place, so no half-written last entry or unfinished save is ever used. Because: the same saved data may hold singers and attendance, which an interrupted save must not damage.
- A11: for lack of space or any other failure the section leader is told the absence was not recorded and that what was saved before is unchanged. It is not retried by itself and a later success is not assumed; they record it again and check the view, or tell the person who plans the programme. Because: a failed save shown as done would leave a missing singer unknown.
- A12: recordings are applied one at a time, each on the latest saved register, with the rules checked again then, so one never overwrites another. This holds for two open copies on the hall computer, and for the attendance commands too. No promise is made for shared or remote storage, which the slice does not use. Because: several section leaders use one computer (000_problem.md line 17), and a lost absence would not be noticed.
- A13: a recording waits for another change to finish for at most 5 seconds. Then it gives up, says the absence was not recorded and that it can be tried again; it is never repeated by itself. Because: changes here are small, and an endless or silent wait would leave the section leader unsure.
- A14: the view reads singers and absences together as they stood at one moment, shows the time it did so, and says that it shows only absences recorded so far, so others may still be missing. With none recorded it says so and does not say everyone is coming. A change made later appears when the view is looked at again, including after reloading, a second window or the computer waking; it does not update by itself. Because: an old or empty view must not be read as a full account of who is coming.
- A15: every absent singer is listed, with no limit on how many; where they do not fit on one screen the rest are reached by scrolling, in the same order. Long names wrap and are never cut. Because: a name left off or cut could hide who is missing among about 45 singers (000_problem.md line 5).
- A17: dates are shown with weekday, day, month name and year in the Gregorian calendar, with ordinary digits and English wording like the rest of the register. Counts read as "2 of 7 known to be missing"; zero reads as "0 of 5", and one and several are worded correctly. Because: a written month and weekday cannot be read as another day, and saying "known to be missing" stops the number being read as who is coming.
- A18: a date is typed in one form only, year-month-day. It is a plain calendar date with no time or zone and is never shifted. Anything empty, unreadable, impossible or in another form records nothing, and the form wanted is shown. Because: one stated form leaves no day and month to confuse.
- A19: slice 1 accepts only Thursdays that have not passed, however far ahead; the confirmation shows the full date so a wrong year is seen. Another weekday is refused with the next Thursday named. Breaks, extra rehearsals and changed days are slice 6. Because: slice 1 covers ordinary Thursday rehearsals (slice-001/004_slice.md line 23), and refusing other days catches a mistyped date before it is saved.
- A20: when recording, a missing or empty date records nothing and asks for one. When looking, no date means the coming Thursday, today's if today is a Thursday, and the view names the date it chose. Because: guessing a date for a record that cannot be undone is unsafe; for looking the next rehearsal is the one wanted and is shown by name.
- A22: on the day the view shows today's rehearsal like any coming one. An unknown name is refused as always. Saved absences stay through reloads, restarts, a second copy and the passing hours; what recording is accepted on the day is the decision above. Because: nothing about looking or keeping needs to change on the day.
- A24: reloading, closing, restarting or time passing neither changes, repeats nor removes a saved absence or a singer; anything not confirmed was not saved. A rehearsal moves to passed only by its date. Because: what was recorded must still be there on Tuesday evening.
- A25: a refused recording keeps nothing, so it changes no view and survives no reload or restart. Each later try is judged afresh. Refused every time, the section leader tells the person who plans the programme. Because: record-for-singer / 2 and 3 record nothing, so there is nothing to carry forward.
- A26: each refusal appears where the recording was made, in plain words in the same text as everything else: what is wrong and what to do. For an unknown name: check the spelling or have the singer added to the register, which it already allows. For a passed date or wrong form: give another date. No internal detail is shown. Because: a refusal with no next step leaves a phoned absence unrecorded.
- A27: a name matches when it is the same ignoring capital letters, spaces at the ends and repeated spaces, and treating the two ways of encoding the same accented letter as one. Compared at each recording; the register's own spelling is what is saved and shown. Accent marks, hyphens, other spellings, word order and missing parts must match. Nothing is guessed. Because: ignoring harmless typing differences helps, while guessing could record the wrong singer.
- A28: any letters and punctuation may be typed. There is no separate length limit: a name matching nobody is refused as unknown. An empty, all-space or missing name asks for a name. Hidden or control characters are never matched away or acted on; the name is handled only as text. Because: the only valid names are those already in the register.
- A29: where more than one singer matches, they are shown with voice part and the section leader says which; if they cannot be told apart, nothing is recorded and they are told why. Names are shown exactly as the register has them. Because: recording the wrong one of two singers would give wrong numbers until someone noticed.
- A30: a singer and a rehearsal date are both required; with either unknown nothing is recorded. The rules about them are held where the absence is saved, whatever way the request arrived, in this order: both given, singer found, date acceptable, yes given. The section leader's own part is not checked by the register in this slice's criteria; see who may record. Because: a rule kept only on the screen can be passed by.
- A31: a recording adds one absence; it cannot set or change a singer's name or voice part, or attendance. Nothing else sent with it is saved. Because: the request needs only a singer and a date.
- A32: at once the section leader sees that the absence is recorded, or why it is not. Going back or starting again records nothing by itself; a new yes is needed, and a repeat is reported as already recorded. Because: nobody should have to guess whether it worked.
- A33: each absence is saved with the date and time it was recorded, by the hall computer's clock, in the same save. That is when it was recorded, not when the singer phoned. Which rehearsal it is for decides where it counts. There is no separate log; refused and failed tries keep nothing. Who recorded it is not known, since nobody is asked to identify themselves. Because: the time helps settle a disputed entry and will be needed for Tuesday evening, while a second log would be one more copy of names and absences.

- A35: tests and examples use made-up names and absences, never anything copied from the register. Because: files kept with the code are copied wherever the code goes.
- A36: the view shows the rehearsal date, time of reading, voice parts, absent singers' names and the counts, and nothing else about a singer. No reason for absence is collected. A recording's answer shows only the singer concerned. Because: planning the programme needs who and which part only.
